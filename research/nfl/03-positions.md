# Positions
Level: basic
Applies to: 2026 season (S1). S2/S3 are undated.

Citation key: see `01-basic-rules.md`. Sources are listed in `sources.md`.

## How many, and who
- Each team has 11 players on the field [S1 R5-1-1, p.23]. Positions fall into offense, defense and special teams [S2].
- S2 groups them as: **offensive line** (center, guards, tackles), **offensive backfield** (quarterback, running backs, fullback), **defensive line** (ends, tackles, nose tackle) and **defensive backfield** (cornerbacks and safeties) [S2].

## Offense
| Position | What they do | Where they line up | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quarterback (QB) | Leads the offense. Takes the snap, then hands off, runs or passes | Backfield, behind the center | [S2 "Quarterback"] |
| Center (C) | Snaps the ball between his legs to the QB; directs the blocking | Middle of the offensive line | [S2 "Center"] |
| Guard (G) ×2 | Blocks pass rushers and opens running lanes | Either side of the center | [S2 "Offensive Guard"] |
| Tackle (T) ×2 | Protects the QB and opens running lanes | Outside the guards, at the ends of the offensive line | [S2 "Offensive Tackle"] |
| Tight end (TE) | Blocks on runs, catches passes on pass plays | End of the offensive line | [S2 "Tight End"] |
| Wide receiver (WR) | Catches passes; known for speed | Out wide, near the sidelines | [S2 "Wide Receiver"] |
| Running back (RB) / halfback / tailback | Main ball carrier | Backfield | [S2 "Running Back"] |
| Fullback (FB) | Blocks for the RB; can also run or catch | Backfield | [S2 "Fullback"] |

### Formation rules (what must be true at the snap)
- At least **7 players on the line of scrimmage** [S1 R7-5-1(a), p.35].
- An **eligible receiver at each end of the line**. Everyone on the line between them is an ineligible receiver [S1 R7-5-1(b), p.35].
- Eligible receivers (and a T-formation QB) wear **0–49 or 80–89**. Offensive linemen wear **50–79** [S1 R5-1-2, p.23].
- The five interior offensive linemen (C, G, G, T, T) are therefore ineligible receivers. They can't go downfield to catch passes unless they report a change of position to the Referee [S1 R5-1-2, R5-3-1, p.23–24].

### Example lineup (illustrative)
One legal arrangement of 11: WR · T G C G T · TE on the line (7 players, with eligible receivers at both ends), plus a second WR off the line, and QB, FB and RB in the backfield. This example just applies the rules in R7-5-1. It doesn't claim to be the most common formation; S3's diagrams of specific offensive formations couldn't be extracted.

## Defense (base "4-3")
S2 describes the 4-3 as the standard defense: 4 linemen, 3 linebackers, and 4 defensive backs (2 cornerbacks + 2 safeties) [S2 "Linebacker", "Defensive Tackle", "Cornerback", "Safety"].

| Position | What they do | Where they line up | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Defensive end (DE) ×2 | Pushes into the backfield to pressure or sack the QB | Outside ends of the defensive line | [S2 "Defensive End"] |
| Defensive tackle (DT) ×2 in a 4-3 | Disrupts plays in the backfield; stops the run | On the defensive line, opposite the guards | [S2 "Defensive Tackle"] |
| Nose tackle (NT) | Disrupts plays in the backfield | Opposite the center (3-4 defense only) | [S2 "Nose Tackle"] |
| Linebacker (LB) ×3 in a 4-3: Mike (middle), Sam (strong side), Will (weak side) | Helps stop the run, covers passes, rushes the QB | **3–5 yards** behind the defensive line | [S2 "Linebacker"] |
| Cornerback (CB) ×2 | Covers the wide receivers | Defensive backfield, across from the receivers | [S2 "Cornerback"] |
| Free safety (FS) | Defends the pass and reads the QB | Deepest player, **10–15 yards** off the line | [S2 "Safety"] |
| Strong safety (SS) | Defends the pass and supports the run | Closer to the line, on the tight end's side | [S2 "Safety"] |
| Nickel back | A **fifth defensive back** for passing situations, replacing a linebacker | Covers a receiver | [S2 "Nickel Back"] |

- Defensive numbers: defensive backs 0–49, defensive linemen 50–79 and 90–99, linebackers 0–59 and 90–99 [S1 R5-1-2, p.23].
- Other defensive fronts S3 names: **3-4** and **nickel** [S3].

## Special teams
| Position | What they do | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Placekicker (K) | Kicks field goals, extra points and kickoffs | [S2 "Placekicker"] |
| Punter (P) | Punts the ball on fourth down | [S2 "Punter"] |
| Long snapper (LS) | Snaps the ball back to the holder or punter | [S2 "Long Snapper"] |
| Holder (H) | Catches the snap and sets the ball for the kicker | [S2 "Holder"] |
| Kick / punt returner (KR/PR) | Catches kicks and tries to run them back | [S2 "Punt/Kick Returner"] |

- Kickers and punters wear 0–49 or 90–99 [S1 R5-1-2, p.23].
- **Who holds?** "In today's NFL, most teams use their punter as a holder"; backup QBs are also used. "It is exceptionally rare for a team to reserve a roster spot solely for a placekick holder" [S6].

### Units [S7]
- **Field goal / extra point:** long snapper on the line snaps to the holder; the holder is "usually positioned 7–8 yards from the line of scrimmage"; the placekicker kicks; offensive linemen block.
- **Punt:** long snapper on the line snaps to the punter, who "usually lines up 15 yards behind the line of scrimmage". The personal protector (upback) lines up "approximately 1–3 yards behind the line of scrimmage" to block for the punter. **Gunners** "usually line up near the sidelines" and "specialize in running down the field very quickly" to tackle the returner.
- **Kick return / punt return:** the returners are "among the fastest players on a team".
- Kickoff unit: not drawn yet, because the 2026 kickoff formation has its own rules (R6-1-2/3). See Open questions.
