# Ties & overtime
Level: basic
Applies to: 2026 season

Citation key: see `01-basic-rules.md`. Sources are listed in `sources.md`. Everything here is Rule 16 [S1 R16, p.72–73].

## When
- Tied at the end of regulation (regular season or postseason) → "modified sudden-death overtime" [S1 R16-1-1, p.72].
- New coin toss right away; the visiting captain calls it [S1 R16-1-2, p.72].

## Regular season
- One extra period of **10 minutes**, after an intermission of at most 3 minutes [S1 R16-1-3, p.72].
- Each team gets **2 timeouts** [S1 R16-1-3(e), p.72].
- **Both teams get a chance with the ball** ("opportunity to possess") once. Exception: if the team that kicks off scores a safety on the receiving team's first possession, the kicking team wins at once [S1 R16-1-3(a), p.72].
- After both have had their chance: more points wins [S1 R16-1-3(b), p.72].
- If the first team doesn't score on its first possession, or it's still tied after both had the ball, **the next score of any kind wins** [S1 R16-1-3(c), p.72].
- **Ties:** at most one 10-minute period, even if the second team never got the ball or its possession hasn't ended. Still tied → the game ends in a tie [S1 R16-1-3(d), p.72].

## Postseason
- Periods of **15 minutes**, with the same possession rules [S1 R16-1-4(a)–(c), p.72].
- Tied after a period, or the second team's first possession hasn't ended → another period, "regardless of how many 15-minute periods are necessary" [S1 R16-1-4(d), p.72]. So there are no ties in the playoffs.
- 2 minutes between periods; teams switch ends after the 1st and 3rd periods; 3 timeouts per team per half [S1 R16-1-4(e)(f)(g), p.72].

## Fine print (both)
- "Opportunity to possess" is about kicks: the kickoff is the receiving team's chance. If the kicking team legally recovers it, the receiving team is considered to have had its opportunity [S1 R16-1-5(c), p.72].
- No coaches' challenges in overtime; replays are started by the Replay Official [S1 R16-1-5(d), p.73].

## Soccer comparison
- Soccer: a match can end drawn ("If both teams score no goals or an equal number of goals, the match is drawn"). When a competition needs a winner, it uses extra time (two periods of at most 15 minutes) and/or penalties [S4 Law 10.2].

## Examples used on the site (illustrative, applying R16-1-3)
- A scores a TD, then B only kicks a FG → A wins (more points after both had the ball).
- A kicks a FG, then B scores a TD → B wins.
- A kicks a FG, B kicks a FG → still tied → next score wins.
- A doesn't score → the next score, by either team, wins.
