# How NFL contracts work
Level: advanced
Applies to: the 2020 CBA (S33) and its NFL Player Contract (Appendix A); official glossary S50. Real example: Lamar Jackson (S51–S53).
Scope (editorial choice, 2026-10-03): the parts of a contract, guarantees, incentives (LTBE/NLTBE), restructures and void years. Signing bonus proration and dead money are in 12-salary-cap.md.

## The parts of a contract
- **Base salary ("Paragraph 5" salary):** pay for the regular season [S50]. "Player will be paid 100% of his yearly salary under this contract in equal weekly or biweekly installments over the course of a 34- or 36-week period", starting with the club's first regular season game [S33 App. A ¶6, p.354–355]. On the cap it counts in the year earned [S33 Art. 13 §6(a)(i), p.125].
  - Guaranteed or not: base salary is only protected if the contract guarantees it (for skill, cap and/or injury) [S50]; otherwise the club can terminate the contract [S33 App. A ¶11, p.356]. Guaranteed base salary stays base salary on the cap: it isn't treated as signing bonus just because it's guaranteed [S33 Art. 13 §6(b)(iii), p.129].
- **Signing bonus:** money for signing the contract [S50]. On the cap it's prorated over the contract, up to 5 years [S33 Art. 13 §6(b)(i), p.126] (see 12-salary-cap.md).
- **Roster bonus:** earned by being on the roster on a set date; counts in full on that season's cap [S50].
- **Workout bonus:** paid for attending an agreed share of the offseason workouts [S50]. Bonuses within the player's sole control (e.g. offseason workout bonuses) are deemed "likely to be earned" [S33 Art. 13 §6(c)(i), p.130], so they count on the cap from the start.

## Guarantees
- By default a contract isn't guaranteed: the club may terminate it if, "in the sole judgment of Club, Player's skill or performance has been unsatisfactory", and, while a cap is in effect, to make room for another player [S33 App. A ¶11, p.356].
- Guaranteed money is pay "protected for skill, cap and/or injury"; it's fully guaranteed only when protected for all three [S50].
- Real example: Lamar Jackson's 2023 deal with Baltimore: 5 years, $260 million, $185 million guaranteed [S52]. $135 million was fully guaranteed at signing, including a $72.5 million signing bonus [S51]. The other $50 million became guaranteed later, in steps (e.g. $17.5 million of his 2024 base salary in March 2024) [S51].

## Incentives: LTBE and NLTBE
- An incentive counts on this year's cap if it's "likely to be earned" (LTBE), judged by the player's and/or team's performance in the prior year [S33 Art. 13 §6(c)(i), p.130]. Otherwise it's "not likely to be earned" (NLTBE) and doesn't count up front [S50].
- Team goals: LTBE if the team met that mark in the prior year, NLTBE if it didn't [S33 Art. 13 §6(c)(iv), p.131].
- End-of-season settle-up [S33 Art. 13 §6(c)(ii)–(iii), p.130]:
  - NLTBE bonuses that were actually earned: if they put the team over the cap, the excess is charged to next year's cap.
  - LTBE bonuses that weren't earned (net of NLTBE ones that were): the difference is credited to next year's cap, to the extent it exceeds the team's room.
- Example (illustrative): a receiver with 900 yards last year gets a bonus for 1,000 yards. NLTBE → it doesn't count now. If he reaches 1,000, it's settled on next year's cap.

## Restructure
- A restructure converts part of a player's base salary (or roster bonus) into a signing bonus: the cap hit drops now, and future cap hits go up [S50].
- Rule behind it: money paid for "contract modifications" counts as signing bonus [S33 Art. 13 §6(b)(iii)(3), p.128], so it's prorated over the remaining years, up to 5 [S33 Art. 13 §6(b)(i), p.126].
- Real example: in March 2026 Baltimore converted $51.3 million of Lamar Jackson's base salary into a signing bonus. His 2026 cap figure went from $74.5 million to $34.54 million (about $40 million of room), and his 2027 cap hit rose to $84.49 million. The deal has two void years [S53].

## Void years
- Years added to a contract so the signing bonus can be prorated over more years, lowering the cap hits [S54]. They don't extend the contract: they're placeholders for the proration [S55].
- When the deal expires and the player leaves, "all of the remaining money in those void years accelerates and hits the cap" [S54]. CBA rule: a contract year the player can end on a contingency counts for proration until the contingency happens; then the amounts for that year are accelerated into Team Salary [S33 Art. 13 §6(b)(ii)(4), p.127].
