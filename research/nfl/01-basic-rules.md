# Basic rules
Level: basic
Applies to: 2026 season

Citation key: `[S1 R11-1-2, p.49]` = 2026 Rulebook, Rule 11, Section 1, Article 2, PDF page 49. Sources are listed in `sources.md`.

## The teams
- Two teams of 11 players each [S1 R5-1-1, p.23].
- The team with the ball is the offense; it tries to move the ball down the field and score. The defense tries to stop it [S2 "Offense/Defense"].
- At the snap, the offense must have at least 7 players on the line of scrimmage, with an eligible receiver at each end of the line [S1 R7-5-1, p.35].
- Having more than 11 players on the field while the ball is in play is a foul: a 5-yard penalty [S1 R5-1-1, p.23].

## The field
- 360 feet long and 160 feet wide, i.e. 120 × 53⅓ yards [S1 R1-1-1, p.8]. In meters (1 yd = 0.9144 m [S5]): 109.73 × 48.77 m. The field of play is 100 yd = 91.44 m, and each end zone is 10 yd = 9.14 m.
- The goal lines are 10 yards in from each end line. Between them is the **field of play**. The two 10-yard areas behind the goal lines are the **end zones** [S1 R1-1-1, p.8].
- Yard lines are marked every 5 yards, and numbers mark every 10 yards [S1 R1-2-1, R1-2-4, p.8].
- **Hash marks** (inbounds lines) are 70 ft 9 in in from each sideline. When a play ends outside them, the next play starts from the nearest hash [S1 R1-1-1, R1-2-2, p.8; R7-6-1/2, p.35].
- The goal sits on the end line: a crossbar 18 ft 6 in wide with its top 10 ft off the ground, and uprights rising 35 ft above it [S1 R1-3-1/2, p.8].

## Game length and the clock
- 60 minutes in four 15-minute quarters (periods) [S1 R4-1-1, p.18].
- Breaks: at least 2 minutes after Q1 and Q3, and 13 minutes at halftime [S1 R4-1-2/3, p.18].
- Teams switch ends after Q1 and Q3. The down, distance and ball position carry over [S1 R4-2-3, p.18].
- **Coin toss:** the visiting captain calls it. The winner chooses either (a) to kick or receive or (b) which goal to defend, or defers the choice to the second half [S1 R4-2-2, p.18].
- **Play clock:** the offense has 40 seconds to snap the ball (25 seconds after certain administrative stops) [S1 R4-6-1/2, p.20].
- **Some things that stop the game clock:** an incomplete pass, the ball going out of bounds, a foul, a change of possession, a score or other dead ball on or behind a goal line, and the two-minute warning [S1 R4-4, p.19].
- **Two-minute warning:** an automatic timeout when the clock passes 2:00 in Q2 and Q4 [S1 R3-41].
- **Timeouts:** 3 per team per half, and 2 per team in a regular-season overtime [S1 R4-5-1, p.19].

## Downs: the core loop
- The offense gets a **series of four downs** to reach the **line to gain** [S1 R3-7-2, p.11].
- The line to gain is 10 yards ahead of where the series started. If the goal line is closer than that, the goal line is the line to gain ("and goal") [S1 R3-7-3, p.11].
- Reaching it earns a new set of four downs, called a **first down** [S1 R7-3-1, p.34].
- Failing to reach it after four downs gives the ball to the other team where the play ended [S1 R7-3-2, p.34]. This is why teams usually punt on 4th down: a **punt** is a 4th-down kick that gives the ball to the opponent farther downfield [S2 "Punt"].
- Progress is measured from the **forward point of the ball** where it was declared dead [S1 R7-3-3, p.34].
- Each play starts with a **snap** from the line of scrimmage [S1 R7-6-1, p.35]. The **line of scrimmage** is an imaginary sideline-to-sideline line through the ball; the two teams line up on opposite sides of it [S2 "Line of Scrimmage"].
- The offense may throw **one forward pass per down**, and only from behind the line of scrimmage [S1 R8-1-2, p.37].

## Moving the ball: run or pass
- **Run:** the QB can hand the ball to a teammate. Handing the ball **forward** is only legal to an eligible receiver who is behind the line of scrimmage [S1 R8-7-4, p.41; R3-14, p.12]. A backfield player such as the RB is an eligible receiver [S1 R5-1-2, p.23]. Whoever is holding or carrying the live ball is the **runner** [S1 R3-27, p.15]. The play continues until the runner is downed, goes out of bounds or scores [S1 R7-2-1, p.33].
- **Pass:** one forward pass per down, thrown from behind the line of scrimmage [S1 R8-1-2, p.37]. If a player catches it before it touches the ground, it's **complete**, and the catcher can keep running [S1 R8-1-3, p.37]. If not, it's **incomplete**: that down is used up and the ball goes back to where the play started [S1 R8-1-4, p.38]. If a defender catches it, it's an **interception** [S1 R8-1-3, p.37].
- The QB takes the snap and moves the ball by running or passing; the RB is the main ball carrier [S2 "Quarterback", "Running Back"].

Example: 1st-and-10 at your own 25. The line to gain is your 35. Gain 4 → 2nd-and-6. Gain 7 → you're at the 36, past the 35 → new 1st-and-10. (Applies S1 R3-7-3 and R7-3-1.)

## Scoring
| Play | Points | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Touchdown | 6 | [S1 R11-1-2, p.49] |
| Field goal | 3 | [S1 R11-1-2, p.49] |
| Safety | 2 | [S1 R11-1-2, p.49] |
| Try (after a TD): kick | 1 | [S1 R11-1-2, R11-3-2, p.49] |
| Try (after a TD): run/pass into the end zone | 2 | [S1 R11-1-2, R11-3-2, p.49] |

- **Touchdown:** a runner with the ball breaks the plane of the opponent's goal line, or a player gets possession of the ball in the opponent's end zone [S1 R11-2-1, p.49].
  - **The ball doesn't have to fully cross the line.** It has to be on, above or behind the plane of the goal line while a runner holds it [S1 R11-2-1(a), p.49]. On the site we say "the tip of the ball reaches the goal line while a player holds it", not "in the air above it", which reads like a thrown ball over the end zone counts (feedback 2026-10-01). A loose ball (fumble or kick) in the end zone scores only if a player gets possession of it there [S1 R11-2-1(d), p.49].
  - Compare soccer: a goal needs "the whole of the ball" to pass over the goal line [S4 Law 10.1]. That's the opposite of what beginners coming from soccer expect.
- **Try:** a single play after a touchdown, run with the game clock stopped. The scoring team snaps from the 15-yard line for a kick or the 2-yard line for a run/pass [S1 R11-3-1, p.49]. If the defense returns the ball for a score during the Try, it gets 2 points [S1 R11-3-2(b), p.49].
  - Names: S2 calls the kick the **extra point** and the run/pass the **two-point conversion** [S2 "Extra Point/Two-Point Conversion"]. On the site we say **1-point attempt** / **2-point attempt** (editorial choice, 2026-10-01), matching the point values in [S1 R11-1-2(d), p.49]. The 2-point version scores exactly like a touchdown: a Try that ends in a touchdown is worth 2 [S1 R11-3-2(b), p.49].
  - "Mini touchdown" as a nickname for the two-point conversion: searched 2026-10-01, no source found, so it's not used on the site.
- **Field goal:** a place kick or drop kick from on or behind the line of scrimmage. The entire ball must pass through the goal (above the crossbar, between the uprights) [S1 R11-4-1, p.50]. No kicking tee is allowed [S1 R11-4-4, p.50].
  - When: on 4th down, a team that fails to gain enough yards for a first down and is too far from the goalposts to attempt a field goal sends in the punter [S2 "Punter"]. So, usually: stuck on 4th down and close enough → field goal; too far → punt.
- **Safety:** typically the offense is downed with the ball in its own end zone, or the ball goes out of bounds behind its own goal line, on a play where the offense put it there [S1 R11-5-1, p.51]. The team that gave up the safety then free-kicks from its own 20 [S1 R11-5-2, p.51]. When the receiving team ends up with the ball after a free kick, it gets a new series of downs [S1 R7-3-2(c), p.34]. So the team that gave up the safety also gives the ball away. (The kicking team can legally recover a free kick [S1 R7-3-1(e), p.34], so this isn't absolute.)
- Whoever has more points at the end wins [S1 R11-1-1, p.49].

## Kicks and restarts
- A **kickoff** starts each half and overtime and follows scores [S2 "Kickoff"]. After a TD+Try or a field goal, the team that was scored on receives the kickoff [S1 R11-3-4, R11-4-6, p.49–50].
- **Touchback:** the ball is dead on or behind a team's own goal line, sent there by the opponent (and not a touchdown) [S1 R11-6-1, p.51]. That team then starts at its own 20. After a kickoff, it's the 20 or the 35 depending on how the kick reached the end zone (next bullet) [S1 R11-6-3, p.51].
  - 2026 kickoff detail: if the kick landed in the "landing zone" before reaching the end zone, the touchback goes to the 20. If it flew into the end zone in the air and was downed (or went out of the back), the touchback goes to the 35 [S1 R6-1-5, p.30].
- **Fair catch:** the returner signals by raising an arm. He may then catch the ball but can't advance it [S2 "Fair Catch"].

## Turnovers
- **Interception:** the defense catches a forward pass. **Fumble:** the ball carrier loses the ball. Either can change possession [S2 "Turnover/Interception/Fumble"].

## Overtime (tied after 60 minutes)
- **Regular season:** one 10-minute period. Both teams get at least one possession, unless the kicking team scores a safety on the first possession. After that, the next score wins. If it's still tied after 10 minutes, the game ends in a tie [S1 R16-1-3, p.72].
- **Postseason:** 15-minute periods with the same possession rules, repeated until there is a winner [S1 R16-1-4, p.72].

## Penalties (overview only)
- Officials throw a yellow flag when a rule is broken [S2 "Penalty/Flag"]. Individual fouls and how they're enforced will be covered later (Rules 12 and 14 of S1).

## Source conflicts
- **Kickoff touchback spot.** S2 says the 25-yard line. S1 (2026) says the 20 or the 35, depending on whether the kick touched the landing zone [S1 R6-1-5, p.30]. **We use S1.** S2 looks outdated.
