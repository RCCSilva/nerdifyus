# How the season works
Level: basic
Applies to: 2026 season

Citation key: see `01-basic-rules.md`. Sources are listed in `sources.md`.

## The league
- 32 teams in two conferences: the American Football Conference (AFC) and the National Football Conference (NFC). 16 teams each [S8].
- Each conference has East, North, South and West divisions, with four teams per division [S8].
- 2026 teams by division [S12] (alphabetical here; the S12 page lists them in standings order):
  - **AFC East:** Buffalo Bills, Miami Dolphins, New England Patriots, New York Jets
  - **AFC North:** Baltimore Ravens, Cincinnati Bengals, Cleveland Browns, Pittsburgh Steelers
  - **AFC South:** Houston Texans, Indianapolis Colts, Jacksonville Jaguars, Tennessee Titans
  - **AFC West:** Denver Broncos, Kansas City Chiefs, Las Vegas Raiders, Los Angeles Chargers
  - **NFC East:** Dallas Cowboys, New York Giants, Philadelphia Eagles, Washington Commanders
  - **NFC North:** Chicago Bears, Detroit Lions, Green Bay Packers, Minnesota Vikings
  - **NFC South:** Atlanta Falcons, Carolina Panthers, New Orleans Saints, Tampa Bay Buccaneers
  - **NFC West:** Arizona Cardinals, Los Angeles Rams, San Francisco 49ers, Seattle Seahawks

## The 17 games [S8]
1. **6** vs. the division rivals: each one twice, once at home and once on the road.
2. **4** vs. all four teams of one other division in the same conference (2 home, 2 road).
3. **4** vs. all four teams of one division in the other conference (2 home, 2 road).
4. **2** vs. one team from each of the two remaining divisions in the same conference (1 home, 1 road). "Matchups are based on division ranking from the previous season."
5. **1**, the 17th game: a non-conference opponent "from a division that the team is not scheduled to play". Also based on the previous season's division ranking.
- 6 + 4 + 4 + 2 + 1 = 17.

## Who makes the playoffs
- Seven teams per conference, 14 in total [S9].
- The four division champions (best overall record in each division) are seeded 1–4 by record. The three wild cards (the best records among the rest of the conference) are seeded 5–7 [S13].
- So a division champion always gets a top-4 seed, even with a worse record than a wild card. A proposal to seed by record was not among the approved 2026 changes [S11].
- Ties in the standings are broken step by step: head-to-head, then division record, common games, conference record, and so on [S10].

## The playoff rounds
- **Wild Card round:** only the No. 1 seed in each conference gets a bye. 2 hosts 7, 3 hosts 6, 4 hosts 5 [S9].
- **Divisional round:** "The lowest remaining seed in each conference travels to the first seed, and the second-lowest remaining seed travels to the second-highest remaining seed." "Home field is determined by seeding number, not position on the bracket" [S13].
- **Conference Championship:** the two survivors in each conference play; the winner is the conference champion [S13].
- **Super Bowl:** the AFC and NFC champions play "at a predetermined site" [S13].
