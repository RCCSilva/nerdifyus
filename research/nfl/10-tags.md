# Franchise & transition tags
Level: advanced
Applies to: the 2020 CBA, Article 10 (S33); 2026 figures (S36, S37)

## Basics
- Each team can put a franchise or transition tag on one player scheduled to become a free agent [S36; S33 Art. 10 §1, §3, p.75–77]. It's a one-year contract [S33 Art. 10 §2(a), p.75].
- 2026 window: February 17 to 4 p.m. ET on March 3 [S36].
- Accepted tags are guaranteed against cuts for skill, injury or cap reasons [S33 Art. 10 §2(c), p.76].

## Real case: Brock Purdy (49ers), 2025
- Before an extension, analysts discussed the 49ers tagging Purdy: tags could keep him off the open market for up to three years [S41]; estimated tag costs about $41M (2026) and $51M (2027), and tagging "would not make him happy" [S40]. CBS warns going year to year risks the player embracing "the franchise tag game and potential freedom" [S41].
- May 2025: 5-year, $265M extension, $53M a year, through 2030 [S42].

## Interpretation (opinion, user's view; site shows it in the Interpretation box)
- Teams tag mainly for money and time: keep a good player without yet committing cap room to the long deal he'd likely earn. The cost is the player's goodwill. Grounded in S40/S41 above.

## Non-exclusive franchise tag
- Salary: the greater of a position average of the five largest salaries (computed as a share of the cap over the previous five years) or 120% of the player's prior salary [S33 Art. 10 §2(a)(i), p.75].
- The player may negotiate with other teams; his team has five days to match an offer sheet; if it doesn't, it gets two first-round picks [S36; S33 Art. 10 §2(a)(i), p.75].
- It is not a trade: the player signs an offer sheet with the new club. The picks are the new club's own (or better) choices, and a club without them available in the upcoming draft may not sign the offer sheet [S33 Art. 9 §3, p.70].
- Timing: compensation is due in that league year's draft, unless the prior club receives the offer sheet later than two days before that draft; then it is due in the following league year's draft [S33 Art. 9 §2(j), p.69–70]. No source found says which year the second pick comes from (the CBA, S36, ESPN, Wikipedia and Bleacher Report all just say "two first-round picks"). Keep the years off the site until one does.
- Real example: Washington tagged DT Sean Gilbert again for 1998; Carolina signed him to an offer sheet and Washington, not matching, received two first-round picks [S38]. Gilbert signed with Carolina on 1998-04-21 [S44], after the 1998 draft (April 18–19) [S45]. Carolina gave its first-round picks in 1999 and 2000 [S43]; the 1999 one was No. 5 [S39]. This fits the timing rule: the offer came after that year's draft, so compensation started in the next draft.
- Not found: a source saying whether any player has left on a non-exclusive tag since Gilbert, so the site doesn't call it "the last time".
- Interpretation (site Interpretation box): expect the next two consecutive first-round picks; it works like a trade at a fixed price.

## Exclusive franchise tag
- Salary: the greater of the average of the five largest current-year salaries at the position or the non-exclusive amount [S33 Art. 10 §2(a)(ii), p.75–76].
- The player cannot negotiate with other teams [S36; S33 Art. 10 §1, p.75].

## Transition tag
- Salary: the greater of the average of the ten largest prior-year salaries at the position (cap-share method) or 120% of the prior salary [S33 Art. 10 §4(a), p.78].
- The player may negotiate with any team; his team has a right of first refusal (five days to match), but no draft-pick compensation [S33 Art. 10 §5, p.79].

## Third time
- Tagged a third time: the greater of the average of the top five at the highest-paid position (in practice QB), 120% of the top-five average at his position, or 144% of his prior salary [S33 Art. 10 §2(b), p.76].
