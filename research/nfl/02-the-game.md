# The game
Level: basic
Applies to: 2026 season

Citation key: see `01-basic-rules.md`. Sources are listed in `sources.md`.

## Length
- 60 minutes, in four periods (quarters) of 15 minutes. If it's tied after four, there's overtime (Rule 16, see `05-overtime.md`) [S1 R4-1-1, p.18].
- At least 2 minutes between Q1–Q2 and between Q3–Q4. **Halftime** (between Q2 and Q3) is 13 minutes [S1 R4-1-2/3, p.18]. So the game has two halves: Q1+Q2 and Q3+Q4.
- Teams switch ends after Q1 and Q3. The down, distance and ball spot stay the same [S1 R4-2-3, p.18].

## The game clock
- The stadium clock is the official time [S1 R4-1-4, p.18]. It counts the time left: the rules speak of "two minutes remaining on the game clock" [S1 R3-41].
- It stops in some situations, for example an incomplete pass, the ball going out of bounds, a foul, a timeout, or the two-minute warning [S1 R4-4, p.19].
- **Two-minute warning:** an automatic timeout when the clock passes 2:00 in Q2 and Q4 [S1 R3-41].
- **Soccer comparison:** "A match lasts for two equal halves of 45 minutes", and the referee adds time for stoppages at the end of each half [S17 Law 7].

## Timeouts
- Three charged team timeouts per team in each half; two in a regular-season overtime period [S1 R4-5-1, p.19].

## The play clock
- The offense must snap within **40 seconds** after the previous play ends [S1 R4-6-1, p.20].
- After certain administrative stoppages (change of possession, a timeout, the two-minute warning, a penalty enforcement, …) it's **25 seconds** from the Referee's whistle [S1 R4-6-2, p.20].
- Too late: delay of game, "Loss of five yards" [S1 R4-6, p.21].

## Reading the TV scoreboard ("score bug")
- In gridiron football, the score bug usually includes the play clock and the down and distance, plus a graphic when a penalty flag is thrown [S14]. It may show the teams' win-loss records [S14].
- On North American TV scoreboards, "the home team and its score are usually displayed to the right of or below the road team's score" [S15].
- Schedules use "at": "New England at Seattle" means New England plays at Seattle's stadium. Real example: the 2026 opener, Seattle hosting New England at Lumen Field on September 9 [S16].
- Down & distance, e.g. "2nd & 8": 2nd down, 8 yards to the line to gain (see `01-basic-rules.md`, Downs).
- **Our example scoreboard** on the site uses made-up numbers and generic "away/home" labels. Showing timeouts as three marks is our design choice: the rule (3 per half) is sourced, but S14 doesn't describe how broadcasts draw them.
